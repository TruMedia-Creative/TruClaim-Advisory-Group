# TruClaims Advisory Group — Brand & Messaging Guide

This file is the source of truth for brand positioning, messaging, voice, audience, and copy decisions for TruClaims Advisory Group.

When writing or editing customer-facing copy, follow this document alongside any applicable copywriting or StoryBrand skills.

---

# 1. Brand

**Name:** TruClaims Advisory Group

**Short Name:** TruClaims

**Industry:** Property Insurance Claims / Appraisal & Umpire Services

**Primary Services:** Independent Property Appraisal and Umpire Services

**Brand Positioning:** Independent. Evidence-driven. Neutral.

**Brand Promise:**

## Fair. Neutral. Resolved.

TruClaims Advisory Group helps resolve disputed property insurance claims through independent evaluation, detailed analysis, construction knowledge, estimating expertise, and structured negotiation.

TruClaims is not an insurance carrier, public adjuster, restoration contractor, or aggressive policyholder advocate.

Its role is to independently evaluate disputed property claims and help the parties reach a fair, supportable, and defensible resolution.

---

# 2. Core Brand Idea

## Bring clarity to disputed property claims.

When two sides disagree about the scope or value of a property loss, TruClaims provides an independent, methodical evaluation of the evidence.

The brand exists at the intersection of:

- property damage
- construction
- insurance claims
- estimating
- documentation
- inspection
- negotiation
- dispute resolution

The central idea is not advocacy.

It is **resolution through objective evaluation.**

---

# 3. Brand Positioning

TruClaims should feel like a sophisticated advisory and dispute-resolution firm.

The brand should communicate:

- neutrality
- balance
- objectivity
- precision
- credibility
- technical competence
- construction knowledge
- professionalism
- resolution

TruClaims should feel capable of stepping into a complicated, high-value property dispute, understanding the evidence, separating facts from positions, and moving the matter toward resolution.

The experience should feel:

**Neutral without being passive.**

**Technical without being inaccessible.**

**Authoritative without being adversarial.**

# 4. StoryBrand Roles

## The Customer Is the Hero

The customer has a disputed property claim that needs to move toward resolution.

Depending on the engagement, the customer may be:

- a property owner
- an insurance carrier
- an attorney
- a commercial client
- another party involved in the appraisal process

The customer does not primarily want "an appraisal."

They want a disputed claim evaluated fairly and moved toward a defensible resolution.

---

## TruClaims Is the Guide

TruClaims brings structure, technical expertise, and independent judgment to the dispute.

### Empathy

Property claim disputes can become:

- complicated
- time-consuming
- expensive
- frustrating
- highly technical
- adversarial
- difficult to move forward

When the parties disagree about scope, pricing, causation, repair methodology, or the value of a loss, progress can stall.

TruClaims understands the complexity of these disputes and provides a structured process for evaluating them.

### Authority

Authority should be demonstrated through:

- detailed inspection
- documentation
- construction knowledge
- estimating expertise
- analysis of scope and pricing
- understanding of property damage
- familiarity with the appraisal process
- structured negotiation
- professional communication
- defensible reasoning

Do not manufacture authority through inflated claims or aggressive language.

Competence should be evident from the process.

# 5. Primary Audiences

TruClaims serves multiple sides of the property insurance ecosystem.

This is important.

Messaging should never imply that TruClaims exists exclusively to advocate for one side.

Primary audiences include:

## Property Owners

Owners dealing with disagreement over the scope or value of a covered property loss.

They want confidence that the disputed loss is being evaluated thoroughly and fairly.

## Insurance Carriers

Carriers seeking qualified, professional appraisal or umpire services grounded in documentation, estimating, and property knowledge.

They need an evaluator whose conclusions can be explained and supported.

## Attorneys

Attorneys representing parties involved in property claim disputes who need technically competent appraisal or umpire expertise.

They value professionalism, documentation, responsiveness, and defensible analysis.

## Commercial Clients

Businesses, property owners, and organizations dealing with complex or significant property losses.

They need a process capable of handling greater scope, complexity, documentation, and financial exposure.

# 10. Services

## Property Appraisal

TruClaims provides independent appraisal services for disputed property insurance claims.

The appraiser evaluates the disputed loss using:

- inspection
- documentation
- estimating
- construction knowledge
- analysis
- professional judgment
- negotiation with the opposing appraiser

The goal is to determine a fair and supportable amount of loss within the scope of the appraisal assignment.

When describing appraisal services, emphasize the quality of the evaluation and the resolution process rather than simply defining the appraisal clause.

---

## Umpire Services

When two appraisers cannot reach agreement, an umpire provides an independent third-party evaluation of the remaining disputed issues.

TruClaims approaches umpire assignments with neutrality, technical competence, and careful consideration of the evidence presented.

The umpire is not there to advocate for either party.

The role is to independently evaluate the disagreement and help bring the appraisal to resolution.

---

# 11. Neutrality Is Non-Negotiable

Neutrality is one of the most important elements of the TruClaims brand.

Copy should never suggest:

- "we fight insurance companies"
- "we maximize your insurance payout"
- "we get policyholders what they deserve"
- "we protect carriers from inflated claims"
- "we reduce claim payouts"
- "we represent your interests"
- "we beat the other side"
- "we negotiate against the insurance company"

Avoid language that frames one side as the enemy.

TruClaims may be retained by a party, but its professional credibility depends on evaluating the disputed loss based on the evidence.

Whenever possible, messaging should reinforce:

**Independent evaluation.**

**Evidence over advocacy.**

**Resolution over conflict.**

---

# 12. What TruClaims Is Not

TruClaims should not be confused with adjacent businesses.

## Not an Insurance Carrier

TruClaims does not insure property or adjust claims on behalf of an insurance company unless a specific service expressly requires otherwise.

## Not a Public Adjuster

TruClaims should not be marketed as a policyholder advocate whose job is to maximize an insurance settlement.

## Not a Restoration Contractor

TruClaims is not selling reconstruction work and should not appear financially motivated by the size of the repair scope.

## Not an Aggressive Claims Advocate

The brand should not use combative "us versus them" claims language.

## Not Merely an Estimating Company

Estimating is an important technical capability, but the value of TruClaims comes from combining estimating with inspection, construction knowledge, evidence, analysis, negotiation, and independent judgment.

# 14. The TruClaims Method

When explaining how TruClaims works, the process should feel disciplined and understandable.

A general conceptual framework is:

### 1. Investigate

Review the claim, inspect the property, examine available documentation, and understand the areas of disagreement.

### 2. Evaluate

Analyze scope, repair methodology, quantities, pricing, estimates, and supporting evidence.

### 3. Resolve

Work through disputed items using documentation, technical reasoning, professional judgment, and structured negotiation.

Exact process language may differ depending on whether TruClaims is acting as an appraiser or umpire.

Do not imply that every engagement follows an identical process when that is not accurate.

# 18. Writing Style

## Prefer

Plain, professional language.

Short paragraphs.

Strong declarative statements.

Specific terminology when technical precision matters.

Clear explanations of complicated concepts.

Active voice.

Evidence-based language.

Examples:

**We inspect the property, review the documentation, and evaluate the disputed scope.**

**Every conclusion should be supported by the evidence.**

**When appraisers disagree, an independent umpire can help move the appraisal toward resolution.**

---

## Avoid

Marketing hype.

Aggressive claims language.

Excessive legalese.

Unnecessary insurance jargon.

Overly emotional language.

Self-congratulatory language.

Generic consulting language.

Generic AI language such as:

- comprehensive solutions
- tailored solutions
- navigate complex claims
- leverage our expertise
- industry-leading
- cutting-edge
- unparalleled service
- maximize outcomes
- seamless process
- trusted partner

These phrases are not automatically prohibited, but they should be replaced with something more specific whenever possible.

# 23. Copy Decision Filter

Before publishing customer-facing copy, ask:

### Clarity
Would the intended audience immediately understand what TruClaims does?

### Neutrality
Could this language make TruClaims sound like an advocate for one side?

### Evidence
Are claims and conclusions grounded in something concrete?

### Precision
Are we saying exactly what we mean?

### Customer Focus
Are we explaining why this matters to the customer?

### Authority
Does TruClaims sound technically competent without boasting?

### Resolution
Does the messaging ultimately move toward resolving the dispute?

### Differentiation
Could this copy belong to any generic public adjuster, insurance company, or restoration contractor?

If yes, rewrite it.

### Action
Does the visitor understand what to do next?

If any answer is unclear, improve the copy before publishing.

# 24. Messaging Tests

Strong TruClaims messaging should pass these tests.

## The Neutrality Test

Could both a property owner and an insurance carrier read this page without immediately concluding that TruClaims is ideologically aligned against them?

If not, reconsider the language.

## The Evidence Test

Does the copy communicate *how* TruClaims reaches conclusions?

Inspection.

Documentation.

Construction knowledge.

Estimating.

Analysis.

Professional judgment.

## The Resolution Test

Does the copy make clear that the purpose of all this technical work is to resolve disputed claims?

Technical expertise is the method.

Resolution is the outcome.

## The Category Test

Could someone mistake TruClaims for:

- a public adjuster
- an insurance carrier
- a restoration contractor
- a roofing company
- a generic claims consultant

If yes, clarify the positioning.
